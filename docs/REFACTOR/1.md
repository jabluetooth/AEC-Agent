Based on your current setup—specifically the goal of automating **MEP/Alarm/LV as-built** digitization and the "disjoint circles" issue shown in your reference image—a standard "trace" algorithm (like Potrace) will continue to fail because it focuses on *contours* rather than *centerlines* and *shapes*.

Here are the best options to optimize your output, ranked from immediate fixes to the robust long-term architecture required for automation.

### 1. The Immediate Fix: "Geometric Primitive Fitting" (Solves the Disjoint Circles)

The issue in your image (left side) is that the vectorizer sees pixels, not geometry. It interprets a noisy circle as a collection of short line segments (a polyline) and breaks them where the scan has gaps.

* **The Strategy:** Do not raster-trace circles. Detect them mathematically first.
* **How to implement:**
1. **Run Circle Detection First:** Before vectorizing lines, run a **Hough Circle Transform** (via OpenCV) on the raster image. This forces the computer to "comprehend" the global shape `[1]`.
2. **Extraction & Erasure:** When a circle is detected, mathematically define its center and radius. **Write this directly as a `CIRCLE` entity** to your DXF output using `ezdxf` `[2]`.
3. **Masking:** "Erase" the detected circle pixels from the raster image (turn them black/background) so the subsequent line vectorizer doesn't try to trace them again `[3]`.



### 2. The Core Pipeline: Skeletonization + Topology Reconstruction

For MEP and wiring diagrams, you need "centerlines" (the path of the wire), not "outlines" (the edges of the ink). Standard vectorizers trace outlines, creating "double lines" for every single wire.

* **Step 1: Morphological Pre-processing:** Use "Dilation" followed by "Erosion" (Closing) to bridge small gaps in the pixel data before processing. This physically connects the disjoint pixels in your raster image `[4]`.
* **Step 2: Skeletonization:** Use `scikit-image.morphology.skeletonize` to reduce thick lines to single-pixel-wide paths. This ensures your output is a single vector per wire, rather than a boxed outline `[5]`.
* **Step 3: Graph-Based Merging (Critical):**
* Convert your vector segments into a mathematical graph using the **NetworkX** library.
* Identify **nodes of degree 2** (a vertex connected to only two edges). These are artificial breaks in a straight line.
* **Merge these edges** into a single continuous entity. This programmatically heals the "shattered" lines seen in your output `[6]`, `[7]`.



### 3. The "Semantic" Option: Hybrid Object Detection (For Automation)

Since your goal is **automation for building plans**, simple vectorization will eventually fail on complex symbols (e.g., Fire Alarm strobes, Valves). A "bag of lines" is not useful for a BIM/CAD workflow; you need "Blocks".

* **The Strategy:** Use a hybrid AI + Classical approach.
* **Component A (Symbols):** Train a lightweight **YOLOv8** model to recognize "Valves," "Smoke Detectors," and "Outlets" as objects `[8]`.
* *Action:* Do not vectorize these. Insert a pre-made AutoCAD **Block** (e.g., `INSERT BLOCK: VALVE` at `x,y`) into the DXF.


* **Component B (Lines):** Use **LSD (Line Segment Detector)** or **Fast Line Detector (FLD)** in OpenCV for the remaining wires and walls. These algorithms are superior to Hough Transform for technical drawings as they better preserve corners and straightness `[9]`, `[10]`.
